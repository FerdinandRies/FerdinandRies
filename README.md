# Ferdinand Ries

**Beethoven scholar and philosopher.** I write on classical music and on the ethics of artificial intelligence, and I build open resources for both fields.

My work follows one rule throughout: *every claim with its source* — who, when, where, and on what evidence. It governs my Beethoven research, my philosophical writing, and the datasets and tools published here.

## Projects

**[Beethoven-Chronology](https://github.com/FerdinandRies/Beethoven-Chronology)** — A source-cited chronology of Beethoven's life and works. Every entry carries a date, a place, and a traceable source, with Jan Caeyers' *Beethoven: A Life* as the scholarly standard. Built for music lovers, checked like scholarship.

**[Well-Argued-Prose](https://github.com/FerdinandRies/Well-Argued-Prose)** — Argument-first standards for top-tier scholarly writing. Fifty-seven rules across nine levels, grounded in a comparative study of twelve articles in *Mind*, maintained as a machine-readable catalog with a command-line toolkit and a 24-point human review protocol. Most tools check your sentences; journals judge your arguments. And if you are tired of the low-quality text AI now produces at scale — fluent, confident, and substantively empty — this framework is the corrective: fifty-seven standards that force every concept to earn its place in an argument.
